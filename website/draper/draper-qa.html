PAUL ANSWERS
YOUR QUESTIONS

Paul Draper kindly took time out from his busy studio schedule in 2006 to answer 38 direct questions submitted by members of the PD-Music forum. Below is the complete 3-page Q&A in full.

PAGE 1 // SONGWRITING, SOLO ALBUM & PRINCE

Q&A PART 1: THE BLANK CANVAS & STUDIO PROCESS

Q (Eclectic Man): Hi Paul, huge fan from the USA here. I was wondering if you're looking to form another band or just go out as a solo artist. Also, as a musician myself, what do you find is the hardest part of the songwriting process: starting a song, or finishing it off?

A (Paul): "Firstly, thank you very much to everyone for taking the interest in the project I am undertaking, after returning from America its great to find people still interested in my writing. At the moment I'm starting this project with a blank canvas, using my own name... I've tried a few musicians out in the studio, with half a mind on a future band to play with, but really its just to get the feel of a good backing track / groove in, and I'm trying to cover most of the instruments myself, a la Prince who is one of my most respected writer/producers. In terms of the songwriting process itself, I find every aspect of it incredibly difficult, only because I've written hundreds of songs and I have a style, so my main objective is to not repeat myself... I've learnt songwriting is a lonely dedication, and patience is the key if you want to be true to yourself and have self-respect as a writer."

Q (Franimall): What are your expectations from your future projects — will you be annoyed if you don't break the Top 40? And what are your albums of the year?

A (Paul): "I'm not interested in charts, only the quality of the songs I write. If I can keep focused on the simple fact that I loved writing songs from the age of 10, then that's what makes me happy and keeps me motivated! On the positive side I love the Arctic Monkeys album, and I'm still playing Bloc Party who I think are brilliant. I like the new Chili Peppers record, The Editors record, and the Arcade Fire album."

Q (Jamesrich): Will your live sets and songs be adapted around a full band, or using synths and samples? Do you expect you'd be playing Mansun songs too?

A (Paul): "I'm making my record which started electronic and has moved more to the live band sound naturally during the recording sessions. Hopefully this means I'm not going to end up like Howard Jones, on my own surrounded by keyboards triggering samples while I'm in the middle throwing off my mental chains!"

Q (Poorshakes): On Kleptomania you wrote that the home demo of Love Is... was more as you intended. Does this mean you were unhappy with the finished version of I Can Only Disappoint U? And what's your favourite Prince song?

A (Paul): "Well I wanted 'Love Is' to sound funky like a Young Americans era Bowie track but with funky loops in it, like I always injected into Mansun tracks, but it was made to sound M.O.R. I just wrote the songs for that record, I can't listen to it, sorry. The demo of 'Disappoint U' was only there to give an insight in how I put the Mansun tracks together — Little Kix was the only album I had to make demos for, the rest I just made up as I went along. Favourite Prince song? 'Something In The Water' off 1999 (the world's best recorded scream) and 'Head' off Dirty Mind."

Q (Reeta): Is there a song by any artist that makes you feel all happy inside?

A (Paul): "Oh yeah!! Kate Bush, 'Wuthering Heights', it's my party piece — I can do the leotard dance from the video, but I have to be with good friends to do it and very out of my tree."

Q (El Diablo): Will the new material be self-produced by you at your home studio?

A (Paul): "I'll be producing all my music from now on in a combination of places!! I'm a control freak unfortunately, and I know that alienated the people I've worked with, some for good reasons, some because they couldn't cut it, but there was nobody else to take the lead in getting things sounding great... I always hear the finished song in my head when I write it, and the recording and producing process is just trying to get to that version in my head."

Q (Candy): What is the longest time you have spent on your own without meeting anyone? Could you sit on your own on Christmas, New Year's Eve and birthdays?

A (Paul): "I've spent weeks or possibly months on my own many times, but I love my own company and I can focus on writing when I'm on my own. I've been on my own on many a birthday / Christmas etc., sometimes thru choice, sometimes not — the others in Mansun did their own thing after gigs, so I would mainly spend the evenings alone with my notebooks, not thru choice I may add! Luckily I've got understanding friends... because as Chauncey Gardiner once said, 'after winter, there is spring!'"

Q (Themanintheapartmentbelow & MrSix): Will you collaborate with anyone else, and what was it like working with Howard Devoto?

A (Paul): "Definitely, I loved working with Skin, she's great, and I loved working with Phil Oakey and Howard Devoto, all people I really respect. Devoto works in a totally different way to me — he's much more lyrical and arrangement, and I'm more a top-line melody movement type of writer, but we cobbled something together in the end."

Q (Face In The Crowd, Hanibul & bbox twenty): Film soundtracks, the name 'Norway Salmon', and how have you changed since Mansun?

A (Paul): "I've got a grand piano and I want to write a movie soundtrack on it one day and also write some classical music, which I've been chipping away at for years. 'Norway Salmon' was on the news about Norway salmon quotas at the exact moment I opened an email account! As for how I've changed: I meditate a lot now, doing that Hare Krishna shit — I'm not Buddhist, but I have a Buddha in my studio and I buy into a lot of Buddhist ideas. I'm calm because I believe if you pass through turbulence in your life and emerge on the other side, you gain that corny 'inner strength' thing."
PAGE 2 // STUDIO VISION, OCD ON 'TELEVISION' & TOURING MEMORIES

Q&A PART 2: HINDSIGHT, VENUES & THE MUSIC PRESS

Q (Poorshakes & Honey Bee): Do you play all the instruments on your solo work, and how do you get through creative blocks?

A (Paul): "Yes, but not all the drums — my drumming can be good enough at times, but my kick drum's always all over the place, so although I'll play most things and produce, I'll have extra bits and bobs of guests on the album. I can't be creative all the time — I've dried up, burned out, fucked up and all the other clichés, but I keep coming back to it. Now I'm working on my own the songs should benefit from having a clear vision without being tripped up in the studio."

Q (NativeMoon): Given the chance, if there was one thing about the path you have taken that you would change, what would that be?

A (Paul): "I think you have no control over your destiny. I've been very loyal to people I shouldn't have over the years, but what goes around comes around and you'll always end up on the path you were meant to be on. My path is writing songs, so no matter what shit gets thrown, that can be regurgitated into a song as far as I'm concerned — everything, good and bad, is an experience to use as a positive outcome."

Q (Divjoy): On Television, were there intentional references to OCD?

A (Paul): "I do go on about OCD in that song coz it's a pain in the fucking arse living with it, it's got some connection to writing or trying to be 'creative' in general. I can't write if I've got things to do, or mundane things to think about."

Q (MrMavis & Marina): Favourite and worst venues, worst band you toured with, and Chad's timekeeping?

A (Paul): "I loved Brixton Academy and Glasgow Barrowlands. Worst venue we played: Glastonbury. Worst band we toured with: The Seahorses. And Chad was always late for everything — even fire alarms in hotels."
PAGE 3 // THE 'PURGATORY' TAPE STORE, GRIND & 'WIDE OPEN SPACE'

Q&A PART 3: THE ARCHIVE VAULTS & THE PARROT SESSIONS

Q (MrMavis): Do you have any Grind recordings or was Steve Heaton left with the masters? Nice lead break in the Epic clip by the way!

A (Paul): "I've got everything I've ever done since I was 10 years old in my tape store that I call 'Purgatory' (however you spell it). I was always a better musician than I let on in Mansun — in the 'Britpop' days you had to be rubbish at playing, but I can string a solo together. In fact 'Wide Open Space' only has me playing on it — it wasn't really a band track, that was one of my solo efforts."

Q (Chronologie): Do you feel time away from the pressure of writing for a band has helped? And will the joint project use the 'Parrot' sessions that Chad and you did?

A (Paul): "I'll only do stuff I'm 100% happy with now, no compromise for me anymore. I don't have the Parrot session, Chad has them — as for working together jointly, he's been helping me out on a few backing tracks on my own project."

Q (RedRedWine): What drives you to make a solo album rather than only writing/producing for others?

A (Paul): "I've wanted to make an album since the band ended — the fact that it's a solo album as such is irrelevant, it's just a way of getting my songs out there into the ether. I'll happily give it away on the net for free if nobody's interested. I'm doing it for me, an element of vanity I suppose if I search deep into my soul, but really just to keep trying to prove myself as a songwriter as that's what I love."
