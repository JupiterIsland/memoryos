16/05/2012

WAYNE RICHES ON WORKING WITH PAUL

Hello all a little update to share which may interest some, (well it did me) after having a chat with Skin's drummer between (2003/2008) Wayne Riches, he was very kind to let the site know a little, about the already very little we know of Paul's solo work, i basically asked him how much work together they did, and if the album was shelved, and how it sounded...

"Hi Richard, Yes i did do a fair amount of work with Paul - initially on the Skin album & subsequently on Paul's solo work. I haven't seen Paul for a year or 2 now but it was great fun working with him & he's a top guy!"
"I don't really know whether it was shelved or not, I did a fair amount of stuff with him then it kinda stopped!? I'm sure he's busy doing something!"
"Its hard to tell because it was a while ago & it really was just a collection of rough grooves & vibes when I left it. It was a bit of both if I remember, kinda guitary but with a bit of a dancy feel - certainly for a few of them.. Can't remember how many tunes we did, was a lot of jamming out riff's & idea's with Paul recording it all to then chop up & arrange at a later date, he's an amazing artist & writer!"

Big thanks to Wayne for sharing this with us, lets hope its developed further and this year we maybe hear something.

http://www.wayneriches.com/

10/11/2006

READ PAUL IN UNCUT MAGAZINE (DECEMBER 2006 ISSUE)

December's issue of Uncut features Paul talking candidly about the break-up of Mansun, the final sessions, and his recovery:

"One night it all kicked off. We broke into the wine cellar, picked out the best bottle of champagne and got twatted. There was a lot of shouting and aggro. Chad punched his hand into a brick wall and ended up in hospital on a morphine drip. People were saying that I was a paranoid schizophrenic. In fact with all the stress I got a malignant tumour and had to have a course of radiation therapy before having it cut out."

16/05/2006

PAUL DIARY ENTRY #2 — ROCKFIELD, BOWENOID MALIGNANCY & DUNCAN FERGUSON

Diary Entry #2 is online. Paul opens up about the chemotherapy sessions he had on his left middle finger around 2000–2001 while recording at Rockfield Studios and how it affected his guitar playing:

"I had a bit of a problem with my 'f--k you' finger (that's the middle finger on my left hand to the uninitiated) - it all started at Rockfield Studios in Monmouth when we were recording 'Getting Your Way', an old Mansun track, and I was really getting into playing the bass, but this sort of blister appeared on my left hand middle finger and the more I played the more it kept bleeding... To cut an even longer story short, I saw a specialist who informed me it was called a Bowenoid Malignancy."

Also (I had to get this in) Paul gives a special mention to Everton legend Duncan Ferguson and the Evertonians cheering his name at the end of Dark Mavis:

"I'd also like to mention the passing of a bit of a hero of mine - the great Duncan Ferguson. The only disappointment of my career so far is not to get a mention on Toffeeweb for giving the Blues their only Number 1-connected record. I got a load of Bluenoses in Parr Street Studios in Liverpool, and I subliminally chanted 'Duncan, Duncan, Duncan Ferguson' at the end of the first Mansun album."

05/05/2004

OFFICIAL STATEMENT FROM PAUL DRAPER ON MANSUN'S SPLIT & ABBEY ROAD VAULTS

"Over the past few months I've been working with Parlophone on pulling together the loose ends since the split of the band. What we have decided is that Mansun's fourth album sessions will be released later this year. These recordings will appear in a three-CD package, entitled 'Kleptomania'. CD1 will be the fourth album sessions, CD2 will have the best stuff from the EPs, and CD3 will be rare and unreleased material that we have dug up from the vaults at Abbey Road... At the point of the band's split, we had completed four sessions for the fourth album, including songs we played on the final tour in 2002 like 'Slipping Away', 'Keep Telling Myself' and 'Getting Your Way'."
